# Mixer screen extension

Mode: Operate. Inherit shipped Bitbounce Membrane. No new visual identity and no raster assets.

Approval: the user selected variant D and then its flexible EQ study for production on 2026-09-21 after reviewing prototypes and iterations. Both studies are archived under `docs/dev/mixer-prototype/` on `codex/mixer-design-prototypes at commit bb506b788996120383411ba6e89ce849d4b1588f`; they are references, not production dependencies. The selected composition has compact channel cards selectable anywhere, equal-height FX cards, larger vertically arranged knob pairs, editable numeric values and draggable Filter/EQ response plots. It omits redundant device explanations.

THESIS: keep channel selection and levels compact so the selected channel's device controls occupy the main working area.

OWN-WORLD: Membrane keeps its charcoal blue-green and pale silver-green themes, Unbounded titles, Barlow controls, IBM Plex Mono readouts, quiet borders and track-color signals. Cards and plots use CSS and SVG.

STORY: select a channel, adjust creative effects and corrective processing, then open Auto Mix when a measured proposal is useful. Master exposes its own creative chain followed by glue compression and a sample-peak limiter. Instruments retains quick volume, mute and solo.

FIRST VIEWPORT: shared transport/navigation, Mixer title with an Auto Mix disclosure, compact channels with Pan inside each track card, then selected-channel processing. Channels and devices scroll horizontally within separate bounded lanes. Pointer input anywhere selects a channel; Enter/Space selects a focused card. The channel-name button retains its pressed state.

FORM: desktop device cards are 308px tall with 5px corners and 10px gaps. Flexible minimum widths are 185px for ordinary effects, 280px for Filter, 390px for EQ and 245px for Compressor. EQ uses a 390px desktop flex basis. At widths up to 600px or with a coarse pointer, cards are 354px tall. Ordinary effects, Filter and Compressor have minimum widths of 250px, 300px and 270px; EQ uses `min(390px, 100vw - 40px)` for both minimum width and flex basis, without growing. Channel cards use a 210px desktop minimum and a fixed 230px phone width; phone internal rows are 44px, 48px and 44px. Cards grow with available space. The page uses the available width and the rack owns horizontal overflow.

Controls: ordinary paired effects stack 76px dials vertically beside labels and numeric fields. Compressor uses 56px dials in three columns; Drive uses one 110px dial. Filter has compact Cutoff and Q values below its graph. EQ has zero to eight independent numbered bands, visible Add/Remove actions, and selected-band Type, Frequency, Gain and Q fields. Every band can change among Bell, Low shelf, High shelf, Low cut, High cut, Notch and Band pass, and move freely across 20 Hz–20 kHz; its number stays stable when bands cross. Gain and Q disable when unsupported by the type. Individual band bypass uses dashed selectors and handles; whole-device bypass remains in the heading. Numbered selectors scroll horizontally on phones. EQ fields use 10px Barlow labels and 11px IBM Plex Mono values. Shift-drag or Shift-arrow adjusts Q where supported. Pointer dragging, keyboard adjustment and exact numeric entry remain available. Phone buttons and graph handles have 44px minimum targets. Focus uses the shared accent outline.

Plots: disconnected biquads calculate the accent Filter/EQ response line. EQ shows the combined enabled-band response plus a muted dashed response for the selected band, whose node has a heavier outline. Its graph is 128px tall. A muted filled trace shows the selected channel's live output spectrum from existing stereo meter taps, separately labeled Output spectrum · dBFS. Bypass preserves the authored response with a dashed line and explicit label. Handles edit the same saved parameters as the fields. Neither graph adds an audible processing connection.

Quality bar: native labeled inputs, keyboard access, stable playback across page changes, readable values and units, both themes and contained horizontal scrolling. Auto Mix retains local analysis, matched comparison playback, undoable Apply, restore and stale-analysis rejection. Full linear arrangement analysis is limited to three minutes. The limiter controls sample peaks; there is no true-peak claim, automation or per-drum submixing.
